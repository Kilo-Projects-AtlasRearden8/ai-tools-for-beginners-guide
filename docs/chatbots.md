# Chatbots

A chatbot is an AI program you can talk to by typing. You type a question or a request, and it types back an answer. It feels a lot like texting a very well-read friend who sometimes makes things up.

Common uses:
- Answering questions ("What's a good dinner for two with chicken and rice?")
- Writing or rewriting emails, letters, and messages
- Summarizing long articles or documents
- Explaining something in simpler words ("Explain how a mortgage works like I'm ten")
- Brainstorming gift ideas, trip plans, or names for a pet

## Free and low-cost chatbots to try

You do not need to pay to get started. Many chatbots have free versions:

- **Google Gemini** — gemini.google.com (free with a Google account)
- **Claude** — claude.ai (free with an account)
- **Microsoft Copilot** — copilot.microsoft.com (free with a Microsoft account)
- **Meta AI** — meta.ai (free in some countries, often inside Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp)
- **Perplexity** — perplexity.ai (good for questions with sources, free tier available)

These all work in a web browser. Some also have phone apps.

## Step-by-step: have your first chat

1. Open your web browser and go to one of the sites above (for example, gemini.google.com).
2. Sign in or create a free account if asked. Use an email address you already have.
3. You will see an empty message box, usually at the bottom of the page, with text like "Ask anything."
4. Click the box and type a question. For example: "What are three easy dinners I can make with eggs and spinach?"
5. Press Enter or click the send button (often a paper plane or arrow icon).
6. Read the answer. If you want to change it, type a follow-up: "Make it quicker, under 15 minutes," or "Give me a shopping list."
7. Keep going back and forth as long as you like. The chatbot remembers what you said earlier in the same conversation.

## Tips for getting good answers

- **Be specific.** "Write a thank-you note" is okay. "Write a thank-you note to my aunt for a birthday gift of $50, keep it short and warm" is better.
- **Tell it who you are talking to.** "Explain this like I'm new to computers" or "Write this for a busy nurse on a 12-hour shift."
- **Ask for one thing at a time.** Complex requests work better when you break them into steps.
- **Say what you don't want.** "No long introductions" or "Don't use medical terms."
- **Ask it to show its work.** For facts, say "Please tell me where you got this information" — then check the source yourself.

## What to watch out for

- **Chatbots can be confident and wrong.** They may invent names, dates, court cases, or medical facts. This is sometimes called a "hallucination," but you can just think of it as "making things up." Double-check anything that matters.
- **They do not know your private life** unless you tell them. Do not tell them passwords, bank details, or health information you would not want shared.
- **Past chats may be saved** by the company. Look in the settings for options like "turn off history" or "don't train on my data" if you want more privacy.

## Try this right now

Copy and paste one of these into a chatbot:

- "Suggest five easy breakfasts that take under 10 minutes and use things most people already have at home."
- "Rewrite this email to sound polite but firm: [paste your email draft]."
- "Explain how a credit score works, like I've never heard of it before."
- "Plan a free Saturday in [your city] for someone who likes walking and old bookstores."

# Image generators

An image generator is an AI tool that makes pictures from words. You type a description — called a prompt — and the tool creates an image that matches it. You do not need to know how to draw.

Common uses:
- Making pictures for a book, a blog, or a social media post
- Creating greeting cards or invitations
- Visualizing an idea ("a cozy reading nook by a window")
- Making coloring pages or simple artwork for personal projects

## Free and low-cost image generators to try

Many tools offer free images every day:

- **Microsoft Designer (formerly Bing Image Creator)** — designer.microsoft.com (free with a Microsoft account, powered by DALL-E)
- **Adobe Firefly** — firefly.adobe.com (free tier with an Adobe account)
- **Leonardo.ai** — leonardo.ai (free daily credits)
- **Craiyon** — craiyon.com (free, simpler results, no account needed to start)
- **Ideogram** — ideogram.ai (free tier, good at text inside images)

These all run in your web browser. Some also have phone apps.

## Step-by-step: make your first image

1. Open one of the sites above in your browser and sign in if asked.
2. Find the prompt box — a blank space that says something like "Describe the image you want to create."
3. Type a description. Start simple: "A golden retriever puppy sleeping in a patch of sunlight, watercolor style."
4. Click Generate, Create, or the arrow button.
5. Wait a few seconds. The tool will show you a few versions of the image.
6. Click an image to see it bigger, then look for a download or save button to save it to your device.
7. Not happy? Change the words and try again. "Make it nighttime," "Add a red umbrella," or "Make it look like an oil painting."

## Tips for better prompts

- **Describe what you see.** Subject, setting, and style help: "A rainy city street at night, neon signs, photo style."
- **Name a style if you want one.** Watercolor, oil painting, sketch, 3D render, cartoon, photo, vintage postcard.
- **Add lighting and mood.** "Golden hour," "soft light," "moody," "bright and cheerful."
- **Keep people simple at first.** Faces and hands can look strange in AI images. Objects and scenes usually come out better.
- **Try again.** The first result is rarely the best. Small word changes often make a big difference.

## What to watch out for

- **Copyright is complicated.** AI-made images exist in a legal gray area. If you plan to sell something that uses AI art, check the tool's terms of service and consider talking to a lawyer. For personal use, most free tools are fine.
- **Some images may include things you didn't ask for.** AI can add odd details or people in the background. Look before you share.
- **Don't generate images of real people without permission**, especially private individuals or medical patients.
- **Avoid making images meant to fool people** (fake news, fake IDs, misleading pictures). That harms real people and can break the law.

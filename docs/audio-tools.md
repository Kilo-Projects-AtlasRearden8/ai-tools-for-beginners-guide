# Audio tools

Audio tools use AI to work with sound. There are two main kinds:

- **Text-to-speech:** the tool reads written text out loud with a natural-sounding voice.
- **Speech-to-text:** the tool listens to someone talking and writes down what they say.

Common uses:
- Having an article or email read to you while you do something else
- Making an audio version of a story or document
- Turning a recorded conversation or meeting into written notes
- Helping with reading if your eyes are tired or reading is difficult

## Free and low-cost audio tools to try

**Text-to-speech (reads text out loud):**
- **Microsoft Edge Read Aloud** — built into the Edge browser. Open any webpage or PDF, click the Read Aloud icon in the address bar. Free.
- **NaturalReader** — naturalreader.com (free tier, reads documents and web text aloud)
- **ElevenLabs Reader** — elevenlabs.io (free tier, very natural voices, app for phone)
- **Apple Books / Google Play Books** — some ebooks have "read aloud" options

**Speech-to-text (writes down spoken words):**
- **Google Docs voice typing** — tools > voice typing in Google Docs (free with a Google account)
- **Apple dictation** — built into iPhone, iPad, and Mac (free)
- **Microsoft Word dictate** — the Dictate button in Word online and desktop (free with Microsoft account)
- **Otter.ai** — otter.ai (free tier for recording and transcribing conversations)

## Step-by-step: have a webpage read to you (Edge)

1. Open Microsoft Edge (it comes with Windows, and there is a Mac version).
2. Go to any webpage or open a PDF.
3. Look for the Read Aloud icon in the top address bar (a speaker or an "A" with sound lines), or right-click on the page and choose "Read aloud."
4. Choose a voice and speed from the menu that appears.
5. Press play. The page reads to you while you listen.
6. Press pause or the X to stop.

## Step-by-step: turn speech into text (Google Docs)

1. Open docs.google.com in Chrome and sign in with a Google account.
2. Start a new document.
3. Click Tools > Voice typing.
4. Click the microphone icon that appears and start speaking clearly.
5. Click the microphone again to stop. Edit the text as needed.

## Tips for good results

**For text-to-speech:**
- Try different voices until you find one you like. Some sound warm, some sound crisp, some sound like a real person.
- Slow the speed down if the voice talks too fast.
- Use headphones for a clearer listen.

**For speech-to-text:**
- Speak clearly and at a normal pace. You don't need to shout.
- Say punctuation out loud: "period," "comma," "new line."
- Use a quiet room. Background noise makes more mistakes.
- Always read what it wrote back — speech-to-text can mishear names, numbers, and medications.

## What to watch out for

- **Voice cloning scams.** Scammers can use short audio clips of a real person's voice to fake a phone call from a loved one asking for money. If someone calls in an emergency asking for money, call them back on a number you already know before doing anything.
- **Don't upload private recordings.** Medical appointments, legal conversations, or family discussions should not be uploaded to free AI tools unless you know they are private and secure.
- **Transcripts make mistakes.** Especially with names, numbers, drug names, and accents. Check anything important against the original recording.
- **Health information.** Audio tools are helpful for reading and notes, but they are not a substitute for a doctor, nurse, or pharmacist.

## Try this right now

- Open a webpage in Microsoft Edge and use Read Aloud to have it read to you for two minutes.
- In Google Docs, turn on voice typing and dictate a short grocery list or a thank-you note.
- Copy a paragraph from this manual into NaturalReader or ElevenLabs Reader and listen to it.

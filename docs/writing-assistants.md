# Writing assistants

A writing assistant is an AI tool that helps you write. It can draft emails, letters, resumes, cover letters, social media posts, or anything else made of words. It does not replace your voice — it gives you a starting point that you can edit.

Common uses:
- Writing or replying to emails
- Drafting cover letters and resumes
- Polishing a letter to a landlord, insurance company, or school
- Creating a book blurb or back-cover description
- Turning rough notes into a tidy message
- Writing a thank-you note, invitation, or apology

## Free and low-cost writing assistants

You can use any chatbot from the chatbots page as a writing assistant. Other options:

- **Grammarly** — grammarly.com (free grammar and clarity suggestions, works in your browser)
- **Hemingway Editor** — hemingwayapp.com (free website that highlights long, hard-to-read sentences)
- **Google Docs** — docs.google.com (has a built-in "Help me write" AI feature with a Google account)
- **Microsoft Word** — word online (Microsoft 365) has an AI writing helper called Copilot with a subscription

## Step-by-step: write your first email with AI

1. Open a chatbot (see the chatbots page) or a writing tool like Google Docs.
2. Tell the AI what to write, including who it's for and what it should say.
   Example: "Write a short, polite email to my landlord asking them to fix a leaky kitchen faucet. Mention that it's been dripping for three days."
3. Read what it wrote. AI drafts can sound stiff or too formal.
4. Edit it until it sounds like you. Change words, add details you forgot, and cut anything that doesn't sound right.
5. Copy it into your email app, add the address, and send.

## Tips for good writing help

- **Give context.** "Write a thank-you note" works, but "Write a thank-you note to my sister for watching my dog while I was in the hospital, keep it warm and under four sentences" works better.
- **Set the tone.** Ask for "polite but firm," "friendly and casual," or "professional and short."
- **Ask for options.** "Give me three different subject lines for this email."
- **Read it out loud before sending.** If a sentence feels awkward when you say it, rewrite it.
- **Never send AI-written medical, legal, or financial claims without checking them.** AI can invent facts.

## Keeping your own voice

AI tends to sound polite and generic. That's fine for a first draft, but your people want to hear *you*. After the AI writes something:

- Swap in words you actually use.
- Add a personal detail only you would know.
- Cut sentences that sound like a robot.
- Sign off the way you normally do.

## Try this right now

Copy and paste one of these into a writing assistant:

- "Draft a polite email declining a party invitation because I'm working that night. Keep it to three sentences."
- "Write a cover letter for a part-time library assistant job. I have customer service experience and love books."
- "Rewrite this text message to sound kinder: [paste your message]."
- "Write a back-cover blurb for a cozy mystery novel set in a small Indiana town."

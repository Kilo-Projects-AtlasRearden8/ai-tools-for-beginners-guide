Safety and privacy basics

AI tools are useful, but like anything on the internet, they work best when you use them carefully. This page covers the most important safety ideas in plain English.

Golden rules

  1. Never share secrets. Do not type passwords, bank account numbers, credit card numbers, Social Security numbers, or private health details into an AI tool.
  2. Assume chats may be saved. The company that runs the tool may keep what you type. Don't write anything you wouldn't want a stranger to read.
  3. AI can be wrong. It can invent facts, names, dates, and medical information. Always double-check anything important — especially health, money, and legal matters.
  4. AI is not professional advice. It is not a doctor, lawyer, pharmacist, or accountant. This manual never gives medical advice. Talk to a real professional for decisions about your health or money.
  5. You can leave at any time. You can close the tab, delete your account, and stop using a tool whenever you want.

Privacy settings worth checking

After you sign in to a tool, look for Settings or your profile icon. Common options include:

  • Turn off chat history — so your conversations are not saved
  • Don't train on my data — so the company does not use your chats to improve its AI
  • Delete my data or delete my account — to remove your information from the service

These options have different names on different sites. Look under "Privacy," "Data controls," or "Settings." If you can't find them, it is okay to just use the tool casually and not share personal details.

Scams to know about

  • Fake AI voices. Scammers can copy a person's voice from a short recording and call pretending to be a relative or friend asking for money. If you get an urgent call asking for money, hang up and call that person back on a number you already know.
  • Fake AI customer support. Real companies will not ask for your password or full credit card number over chat.
  • Too-good-to-be-true images or stories. AI can make fake pictures of events that never happened. Check the source before you believe a shocking image or claim.

Health information: extra caution

AI tools can explain general health topics in plain language, but they do not know your medical history, your medications, or your body. Never use an AI tool to:

  • Diagnose a symptom
  • Change a medication or dose
  • Decide whether to skip a doctor's appointment

When in doubt, call your nurse, doctor, or pharmacist. AI is education only — never medical advice.
